What makes Pectobacterium pathogenic — and where those genes come from

Virulence Factor Inventory (Paper 2: P. aroidearum L6)

Xu et al. (2021) annotated the complete genome of L6 and predicted 478 pathogenesis-related genes (10.4% of genome) via VFDB. The arsenal centers on three pillars:

Figure 3. Left: Toxins (25), PCWDEs — pectinases (28), cellulases (3), proteases (4). Right: 122 secretion system genes across 6 types. T3SS alone has 723 predicted genes, but effectors remain unknown.

Plant Cell-Wall Degrading Enzymes (PCWDEs)

  • Pectinases (28): Main weapons — degrade pectin, the "glue" of plant cell walls
  • Cellulases (3): Target cellulose backbone
  • Proteases (4): Degrade structural proteins
  • These enzymes cause the maceration ("soft rot") symptom

Secretion Systems — Delivery Machines

  • T1SS (14): Direct toxin/enzyme export
  • T2SS (23): Major PCWDE secretion route
  • T3SS (29 structural + 723 associated): Injects effectors into plant cells
  • T4SS (25): Conjugation, DNA/protein transfer
  • T5SS (0): Absent in L6
  • T6SS (31): Interbacterial competition

Horizontal Gene Transfer: The Innovation Engine (Paper 1)

Yang et al. (2026) identified 15 Horizontally Acquired Islands (HAIs) in PccS1 (P. aroidearum) using three GI prediction tools (IslandPick, SIGI-HMM, IslandPath-DIMOB). These HAIs are not random — they concentrate virulence and adaptation genes.

Figure 4. 15 HAIs distributed along the PccS1 chromosome. Color-coded by function: prophages (gold), T3SS/T4SS (red/orange), pathogenicity (red), metabolic (green), AMR (orange/purple). Three complete prophages (HAI1, 8, 13).

What HAIs Carry

  • Secretion systems: T3SS (HAI3), T4SS/virB (HAI12), T2SS (HAI14)
  • Toxins & PCWDEs: HAI6, HAI9, HAI14, HAI15
  • LPS/glycan biosynthesis: HAI10 (surface adaptation)
  • Metabolic islands: Macrolide, terpenoid, polyketide, thiamine (HAI3, 7, 9)
  • AMR: MdtN, MdtA, antitoxin (HAI5, 7, 15)

Three Species Where HGT Drives Pathogenicity

  • P. aroidearum PccS1 (this study): 15 HAIs
  • P. atrosepticum SCRI1043: GIs linked to virulence
  • P. wasabiae SCC3193: Strain-unique GIs for virulence, secretion, colonization (Nykyri et al. 2012)

Conclusion: HGT is a major driver of host adaptation and virulence evolution in Pectobacterium.

Key Takeaways

  • Three pillars: Toxins + PCWDEs + Secretion systems = soft rot machinery
  • T3SS is the crown jewel: 723 genes predicted in L6, but zero effectors characterized — major gap
  • HAIs = innovation hotspots: Phages, integrases, transposases mediate acquisition
  • Convergent evolution: Independent HGT events in different species yield similar virulence toolkits
